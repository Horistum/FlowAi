# v0.9.7.7 Universal Control and Policy Requirements

## Status

Completed.

## Objective

Represent control and safety requirements independently from implementation mechanisms, observed evidence and authorization decisions so equivalent intent retains the same control meaning across inventories and targets.

## Implemented contract

- `ControlRequirement` describes the target-neutral obligation and its semantic source
- `ControlEvidence` records `SATISFIED`, `UNSATISFIED`, `UNKNOWN` or `DYNAMIC` evidence without changing the requirement
- `ControlDecision` derives `ALLOWED`, `BLOCKED` or `PENDING` from explicit evidence
- missing evidence is represented as `UNKNOWN` and blocks authorization
- explicit negative evidence is `UNSATISFIED` and blocks authorization
- dynamic evidence remains `PENDING` and declares the capabilities required for later enforcement
- canonical capabilities and intent policies derive requirements before implementation binding
- module safety contracts may add concrete obligations but cannot remove or rewrite canonical intent requirements
- approval policy declarations no longer count as proof that an approval mechanism exists
- raw Flow safety declarations remain explicit evidence rather than target-specific semantic meaning

## Pipeline integration

Canonical requirements enter `CanonicalIntentMeaning`, authored evidence and decisions enter Flow AST, and planning combines them with module-owned obligations. Execution plans preserve all three contracts. `MandatoryMaterializationAuthority` re-derives canonical and module requirements, validates evidence cardinality and provenance, checks dynamic enforcement capabilities, re-computes the decision and rejects unresolved execution candidates before provider invocation.

Diagnostic materialization preserves blocked control facts as non-executable compatibility evidence. It does not convert unknown evidence into target readiness.

## Compatibility and migration

`MandatorySafetyPolicy` and `SafetyPolicyValidator` remain deprecated compatibility facades over `CanonicalControlRequirementAuthority`; they are not separate production authorities. Existing consumers must read `controlRequirements`, `controlEvidence` and `controlDecision` separately. An `APPROVAL` requirement is not equivalent to satisfied approval evidence, and a `PENDING` decision is not executable authorization.

The reference secret-rotation scenario now contains an explicit `APPROVE` step before secret rotation instead of relying on an approval policy declaration as evidence.
